# Research Ethics and Attribution Policy

## Purpose

The Cypro-Minoan Open Corpus is built on scholarship produced by archaeologists, epigraphers, palaeographers, linguists, museum staff, excavators, photographers, editors, and other researchers. Open computational research does not erase their intellectual contribution.

## Non-negotiable principles

### 1. Credit follows the claim
Every externally derived reading, identification, date, find context, classification, restoration, sign correspondence, or interpretive claim must be traceable to a source. Bibliography alone is insufficient when a record contains source-specific assertions.

### 2. Do not disguise copying as normalization
Changing punctuation, identifiers, character encoding, whitespace, or file format does not transform a published transcription into independent research. A normalized transcription derived from an edition must retain attribution to that edition.

### 3. Facts, observations, and interpretations are different
Records distinguish:
- `published`: asserted in an identified source;
- `independent_observation`: newly recorded from lawfully accessible primary evidence, with method documented;
- `project_derived`: computed or inferred by this project from identified inputs.

No `project_derived` result may be presented as a published scholarly consensus.

### 4. Preserve scholarly disagreement
When responsible sources disagree, retain competing assertions with attribution. Do not silently select a preferred reading merely to simplify computation. A normalized project field may exist only when its derivation and uncertainty are explicit.

### 5. Respect copyright and database rights
A citation is not permission to redistribute. Do not commit copyrighted photographs, plates, drawings, scans, substantial protected text, or bulk editorial transcriptions unless their reuse status is documented. Link/cite when redistribution is not clearly permitted.

### 6. Primary evidence does not erase editorial labor
Even where an ancient inscription itself is not subject to modern copyright, a scholar's drawing, transcription, restoration, commentary, sign classification, or edition can embody modern intellectual and creative work. Attribute it.

### 7. No decipherment by database fiat
The corpus must not encode speculative phonetic values or linguistic interpretations as ground truth. Hypotheses belong in explicitly labelled analytical layers.

### 8. Corrections are transparent
Correct errors through version-controlled commits. Preserve enough history to determine what changed, why, and on whose evidence.

### 9. Contributors cite upstream scholarship
Downstream users should cite both this project and the original scholarship materially used in their work. We will make that easier by carrying citations at record/assertion level.

### 10. Good-faith correction process
Researchers and rights-holders are invited to open an issue when attribution, representation, permissions, or scholarly nuance needs correction. Such reports should be treated respectfully and investigated on the evidence.

## Minimum provenance for a scholarly assertion

Each assertion should identify: `source_id`, locator when available (page/catalogue/figure), assertion type, responsible agent when known, confidence/uncertainty, and any transformation performed by this project.

## AI-assisted work

Machine assistance may help with formatting, validation, search, or analysis, but it is not a scholarly source. AI output must never be used to manufacture citations, fill unknown readings, or silently replace inspection of the referenced scholarship. Human-verifiable sources remain the evidentiary basis of the corpus.
